# Revenue Opportunity Scorecard

Use this as a prioritization aid, not a forecast. Rate each dimension from 0–5 and record evidence beside the score.

| Dimension | Weight | A high score means |
|---|---:|---|
| Evidence strength | 25% | Multiple recent, independent, source-linked observations show the problem exists |
| Buyer urgency | 20% | The problem is frequent, costly, time-sensitive, or blocks an important outcome |
| Willingness to pay | 20% | People already pay for alternatives, request paid help, or show a credible purchase signal |
| Audience reachability | 10% | A specific audience can be reached through a free, permitted channel |
| Time to validate | 10% | A meaningful test can be run within days, not weeks |
| Cost | 10% | The test and initial delivery can run using available/free tools with known costs |
| Delivery simplicity | 5% | A small, repeatable deliverable can be provided with low support burden |

## Calculation

For each dimension, calculate (rating divided by 5) multiplied by the weight; add the weighted points for a total out of 100. If evidence is missing, score it low rather than filling gaps with assumptions.

## Interpretation

- **80–100:** Candidate for a small validation test; not proof of demand.
- **60–79:** Narrow the audience or strengthen evidence before building.
- **40–59:** Keep as a hypothesis; do not invest in automation yet.
- **0–39:** Defer unless new evidence changes the case.

## Mandatory evidence notes

For every candidate record:
- **Observed evidence:** source, date if available, what it says/shows, and whether sources are independent.
- **Inference:** what the observed evidence suggests.
- **Hypothesis:** what still needs to be proven.
- **Counterevidence:** competing products, weak purchase intent, or signals that would invalidate the idea.
- **Validation test:** one observable signal, success threshold, timebox, and stop condition.
- **Cost:** upfront and recurring costs; use “unknown” when not verified.
- **Risk:** privacy, platform rules, regulated claims, operational complexity, and support burden.

Do not count likes, impressions, vague enthusiasm, or unverified sign-ups as sales. Do not report revenue without a verified transaction and appropriate accounting for refunds and fees.
