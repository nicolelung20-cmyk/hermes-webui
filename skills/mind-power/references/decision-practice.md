# Mind Power: Decision and Practice Reference

## Useful mental models
- **Implementation intention:** “When situation X occurs, I will do action Y.” This helps bridge intention and behavior.
- **Opportunity cost:** every active priority delays something else; name the trade-off.
- **Reversibility:** test reversible choices quickly; slow down for irreversible commitments.
- **Base rates:** compare a belief with typical outcomes before treating a vivid story as predictive.
- **Premortem:** imagine the plan failed, identify likely causes, and add one prevention.
- **Feedback loops:** choose a measure that can change the next decision.
- **Environment design:** make the desired action easier and distractions harder to access.
- **Minimum viable action:** define the smallest version that preserves the core benefit.

## Five-minute reset
1. Write the desired outcome in one sentence.
2. Write the single most important constraint.
3. Pick one action that takes ten minutes or less.
4. Remove one source of friction.
5. Start, then record the result without judgment.

## Evidence and uncertainty
Use high/medium/low confidence labels when helpful. Do not turn intuition into proof. A single success or failure rarely establishes causation. When outcomes depend on markets, other people, health, or chance, explain the limits of personal control.

## Financial and business guardrails
- Never promise guaranteed or infinite returns.
- Prefer customer discovery, prototypes, and small validated offers over expensive speculative builds.
- Do not treat a mindset exercise as authorization for a trade, purchase, deployment, or disclosure.
- Separate idea generation from execution approval.
